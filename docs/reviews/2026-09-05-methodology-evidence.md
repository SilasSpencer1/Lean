# Methodology, books, and supporting repository evidence

Inspected on 2026-09-05. These entries supplement the empirical and platform matrices. Book evidence means the specified publisher excerpt, not the complete book. Recommendations in the final column are this review's inferences. No empirical return claim below is evidence of profitable SPY option trading.

| Source / quality and inspected location | Data, instrument, horizon | Method, execution, validation and reported result | Transfer / limitation | Design decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, *The Probability of Backtest Overfitting*](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf), author-hosted February 2015 paper version; §§2.2, 4–6, printed pp.10–12, 21–26. Peer-reviewed methodological work. | Illustrative 1,000-day simulated random walk; 8,800 seasonal rule combinations. No options data or observation years. | CSCV compares the selected strategy's relative out-of-sample rank across complementary partitions. Example selects entry day, holding period, stop and side; in-sample annualized Sharpe 1.27, PBO about 55%, about 53% negative OOS Sharpes. No executable options fills, contract rule, or sizing experiment. | Strong selection-bias evidence, no alpha evidence. §§5.1–5.2 warn about temporal dependence, incomplete trial disclosure, bad costs, unseen regimes and optimizing PBO itself. | Keep a complete experiment ledger. Use chronological evaluation as primary; PBO only a secondary diagnostic if trial count/history support it. Do not manufacture many weak strategies to improve its denominator. |
| [Bailey & López de Prado, *The Deflated Sharpe Ratio*](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf), July 2014 author manuscript for *Journal of Portfolio Management*; pp.7–10 and Appendix 3. | Theoretical selection model and illustrative Treasury seasonality example with five years / 1,250 daily observations. No SPY contracts or actual historical study period. | Adjusts Sharpe significance for trial multiplicity, variance across trials, sample size, skew and kurtosis. Illustrative Sharpe 2.5 does not clear 95% after adjustment. Contract selection, transaction costs, exits and sizing are not an options strategy specification. | Useful warning about selected noisy results; effective independent trial count is estimated, and autocorrelated sparse option returns complicate inference. | Record every experiment and uncertainty; retain Sharpe as secondary. Do not use DSR as a substitute for correct timestamps, execution replay or fresh holdout data. |
| [Ernest Chan, *Algorithmic Trading*, chapter 1, publisher excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781118460146.excerpt.pdf), inspected pp.3–5 and 8–11, “Common Pitfalls” and market-data sections. Respected practitioner book, lower evidence weight than empirical research. | General backtesting examples across markets, no directly applicable SPY intraday dataset or trading experiment. | Discusses reused holdouts, excessive parameters, shared simulation/execution code, survivorship and venue-specific prices. No validated SPY contract rule, costs, exits, sizing, or profitability result in the inspected sections. | Supports simplicity and matching data semantics. Its suggestion that shared code eliminates look-ahead is too strong: external revised data, preprocessing, and event availability can still leak. Do not adopt dated vendor recommendations. | Keep shared rules, but also test causal data replay. Freeze thresholds before holdout inspection. Prefer linear baselines without assuming nonlinear methods can never help. |
| [Euan Sinclair, *Volatility Trading*, second edition, chapter 1, publisher excerpt](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/9781118347133.excerpt.pdf), inspected pp.1–10, especially equations 1.3–1.9 and assumptions. Respected options practitioner book. | Pricing derivation and delta-hedged option examples; no empirical sample, validation split or reported trading return. | Separates gamma, theta, financing and volatility effects; makes continuous trading, liquidity and friction assumptions explicit. Discusses dividends, discrete trade sizes and transaction costs. The hedged construction differs from an unhedged single option. | Appropriate for exposure interpretation, not a claim that forecasting realized volatility monetizes a long SPY call/put. Greek units and model assumptions matter. | Predict option P&L; keep IV/Greeks with provenance. Attribute realized P&L to direction/volatility/decay diagnostically; do not infer an executable edge from a pricing identity. |
| [scikit-learn `TimeSeriesSplit` documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html) and [version 1.7.2 `sklearn/model_selection/_split.py`](https://github.com/scikit-learn/scikit-learn/blob/1.7.2/sklearn/model_selection/_split.py), `TimeSeriesSplit._split`. Maintained primary code/docs; historical tag inspected deliberately, not asserted latest. | General ordered arrays; no market dataset or execution experiment. | Expanding chronological training; `gap` removes a count of rows before test start. No event-interval, label-maturity or contract grouping logic. | Row gaps are insufficient for irregular candidate observations or variable exits. This is an API fact, not proof of a finance validation method. | Offline splitter must work on session groups and actual information intervals; ordinary sklearn preprocessing must be fitted only within training folds. Keep sklearn outside the runtime core. |
| [exchange_calendars `exchange_calendars/exchange_calendar_xnys.py`](https://github.com/gerrymanoim/exchange_calendars/blob/master/exchange_calendars/exchange_calendar_xnys.py), class attributes and regular/ad-hoc holiday lists inspected. Maintained open-source implementation; moving master inspected on review date. | XNYS historical holiday/early-close rules including exceptional closures. No strategy, fill assumptions or performance result. | Provides timezone-aware session definitions, 09:30/16:00 regular hours and 13:00 early close, with historical exceptions. | A library calendar is a versioned implementation of exchange rules, not sole authority for SPY options or broker cutoffs. Even comments can be wrong; verify dates against official schedules. | Resolve sessions outside core, pin dependency/calendar snapshot, compare official stock/options/broker hours, use conservative common session. Add DST/exceptional-closure fixtures. No pandas dependency in core. |

Additional implementation evidence: [Python 3.11 sqlite3, transaction control and connection parameters](https://docs.python.org/3.11/library/sqlite3.html#transaction-control), and [SQLite atomic commit, §§1–2, 8–9](https://www.sqlite.org/atomiccommit.html), were inspected. These primary engineering documents establish explicit transaction/commit and lock-timeout behavior and describe crash testing and filesystem assumptions. They contain no market dataset, trading method or economic result. They support a native local event/reservation transaction instead of a custom crash-atomic JSONL promise; actual local filesystem behavior and failure recovery still need the planned tests. This is engineering guidance only.

The inspected books disagree in emphasis with empirical nonlinear-model results only superficially: simpler models reduce search freedom, while nonlinear models can help on particular large datasets. Neither establishes that this platform needs nonlinear models. The falsifiable choice is to test a predeclared linear baseline first under the actual execution policy.

The PBO/DSR papers do not supply this review's numerical observation floors or operational thresholds. Those are proposed engineering and research acceptance policies, identified as such in the main report.
