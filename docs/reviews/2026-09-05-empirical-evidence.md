# Empirical evidence annex: intraday long SPY options

Reviewed on 2026-09-05. The complete request, 264-line system design, and 1,572-line Phase 1 plan were read before conclusions. Phase 1 is treated as a shared-core implementation, not the complete trading platform. This annex supplies empirical evidence to the main review; it does not recommend orders or establish profitability.

The evidence below distinguishes inspected methods/results from inaccessible material. Page numbers are printed manuscript pages unless identified as PDF pages. Source summaries are deliberately compact; unavailable fields are recorded rather than inferred. Neither whole books nor entire papers are claimed to have been read. The inspected portions are named precisely.

## Inferences and falsifiable design implications

These recommendations are reviewer inferences about the supplied documents, not estimates imported from the papers.

1. **Preserve the no-trade champion and ask-to-bid target.** The project must demonstrate a conditional premium gain that survives spread, fees, delay and missed execution. Evidence for hedged cross-sectional spreads, contemporaneous stock price impact, or gross open-to-close returns answers different questions. No inspected source validates the exact 7–21 DTE, 0.40–0.60 delta, five-minute decision, one-contract, 30-minute holding combination.
2. **Bind labels to actual strategy timing.** The design starts the exit clock at complete fill, while the Phase 1 quote label starts at observation time. Distinguish a diagnostic quote-at-decision label from realized/replayed policy P&L. A delayed entry must shift the 30-minute exit deadline; safety exits, canceled entries, missing exit bids and pending liquidation must remain visible in policy evaluation. Missing exits require censoring reasons and conservative economic bounds; simply discarding difficult observations can improve apparent returns.
3. **Separate contract ranking from expected-dollar economics.** With exactly one contract, a higher percentage return can produce fewer dollars. For illustration, predicted 2% on a $100 premium is $2, whereas 1% on $500 is $5. Freeze whether the objective is net dollars, premium-normalized return, or a preregistered capital-utilization objective. Keep all existing capital caps. If percentage return remains the score, state that choice and test it against the dollar benchmark using training data only.
4. **Make cost scenarios monetary and reproducible.** The current $1 round-trip cost plus constant 0.005 return buffer translates into different dollar reserves across premiums. Store cost-model ID, bid/ask observations, entry/exit delay, per-side fees and adverse price adjustments. Avoid deducting a cost twice when moving from base labels to adverse decisions. Stress delayed entry at the next available ask and exit at the subsequent eligible bid, with canceled opportunities retained.
5. **Use as-of observations for all feature construction.** Train-only transformations, same-contract rolling option histories, and explicit source/availability timestamps are necessary. No contract may enter the historical universe because it has later-complete data. No same-bar high/low, end-of-bar volume, revised event record or future Greeks may appear in an earlier decision. Quote age alone is insufficient to prove when a bar was available.
6. **Keep advanced features optional.** A point-in-time IV-minus-realized-volatility feature and contemporaneously constructed skew/term proxies are reasonable challengers, with explicit units and maturity alignment. A whole implied distribution or order-book signal requires demonstrably sufficient genuine quotes and sizes; two chosen candidates or minute bars do not reconstruct the chain or order flow. Test additions by ablation against the frozen baseline and cost model.
7. **Separate scientific and operational gates.** Thirty paper sessions establish an initial operational observation window; at three entries/day they permit at most 90 entries and do not establish economic significance. A six-month holdout has at most about 378 entries before cash decisions. Minimum sessions and trade counts must be supplemented by dependence-aware uncertainty, effect-size/power requirements and complete attempted-model accounting; no fixed count guarantees evidence.
8. **Publish invalidation rules in advance.** Stop the economic claim if apparent edge depends on midpoint fills, forward completeness, a few sessions, understated exit losses, or unavailable data rights. A technically correct cash decision is a successful platform outcome. Continuing research requires stable positive out-of-sample policy P&L under the approved conservative cost assumptions, not forecast accuracy alone.

## Source-version and access cautions for integration

- Use the university Bali manuscript's Table 6, not its older CFR draft or its internally different introductory summary. Exact sample counts also vary between the newer manuscript's prose and Table 1; this annex intentionally avoids presenting one as authoritative.
- The source matrix distinguishes a primary draft from the final journal article. Peer-reviewed publication status does not imply that an inaccessible final version's methods were read.
- The E3 completeness problem is a methodological concern inferred from an explicit filter; the direction and magnitude of its economic bias were not measured here.
- Books, official broker/exchange documentation, and software repositories are covered elsewhere in the main review. This annex makes no claim to have inspected them.
